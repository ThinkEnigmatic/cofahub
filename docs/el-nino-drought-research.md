# El Niño Droughts in the Freely Associated States — Research Synthesis

> Compiled 2026-10-29 by a parallel research swarm (5 agents). Every figure is
> quoted from a cited source; nothing is invented. Where sources conflict, both
> values are shown. This is contextual research for COFA FORWARD's anticipatory
> drought intelligence — **not a forecast and not operational guidance.**

## Bottom line

The Freely Associated States (Palau, FSM, RMI) sit in the western-Pacific "warm
pool." During El Niño the Walker circulation's rising branch shifts east of the
date line, imposing **subsidence over the warm pool** and suppressing the deep
convection that produces the region's rainfall. The result is a recurring,
well-documented **drought that peaks in the dry season the year *after* a strong
El Niño** (the "El Niño Year +1" / post-peak phase). The region's **low coral
atolls** (most of the RMI, the FSM outer islands, Palau's remote atolls) are the
most vulnerable because they have no surface water and only a thin freshwater
lens + rain catchment.

**Immediate relevance (Oct 2026):** CPC's ENSO Diagnostic Discussion (8 Oct 2026)
is at **"El Niño Advisory"** — Niño-3.4 at **+2.1 °C** and strengthening, with a
strong-to-very strong El Niño likely through Jan–Mar 2027 and a 54–83% chance of a
*historic* event exceeding any since 1950. This is the drought precursor pattern.

## The physical mechanism

1. **Walker circulation shift.** El Niño moves the warm pool eastward; the rising
   (convective) branch of the Pacific Walker cell follows it east of 180°, and the
   sinking (subsidence) branch settles over the Maritime Continent / western warm
   pool (NOAA Climate.gov; Xiao et al. 2022, *Sci. Rep.*).
2. **ITCZ + SPCZ displacement.** The ITCZ shifts equatorward/eastward; the SPCZ
   shifts north and east (up to ~10° of latitude in strong events), leaving the
   FAS sector under the subsiding branch (Cai et al. 2012; Brown et al. 2021).
3. **Rainfall dipole.** The west Pacific dries while the central/eastern Pacific
   gets wetter. In Jan–Mar 1998 the western-Pacific rainfall total north of the
   equator was **<100 mm, >800 mm below normal** (CPC ENSO Rainfall).
4. **Lag.** Drought typically peaks **after** the El Niño peak — in the dry season
   of the following year (PEAC: "a very dry first-half of a calendar year is
   typical of the year following El Niño"). USGS: sharpest drought for Guam/Saipan/
   Chuuk arrives "the year after El Niño onset." Hydrological recovery can lag the
   rainfall recovery by years.

## The three benchmark events

### 1997–98 (strongest in local memory)
- **Timing:** dry from late 1997 (outer islands from Oct); peak **Jan–May 1998**;
  Majuro drought ended **June 1998**.
- **Rainfall:** Majuro (RMI) got **8% of normal rain Jan–Apr 1998**. Jan 1998
  %-of-normal: Kolonia/Pingelap/Nukuoro (Pohnpei) 4–5%, Kosrae 9%, Weno 12%,
  Lukunoch (Chuuk) 3%, Kwajalein/Ebeye 8%, Wotje 18%, Saipan 25%, Koror 44%.
- **Gradient:** Pohnpei/Kosrae and the northern RMI atolls hardest; Palau was last
  to dry (Koror 44% in Jan); southern Palau (Peleliu) was wetter.
- **Crisis:** FSM + Pohnpei + Chuuk declared emergencies; ~28,800 people (~30% of
  outer-island population) needed relief; rivers dried, wells turned saline; taro
  failed; RMI drought disaster Feb 1998; Guam's worst drought (65% of normal annual
  rainfall, 12% of land scorched).

### 2015–16 (super El Niño, record ONI)
- **Timing:** onset Jan–Jun 2015, peak Jul 2015–Feb 2016; drought rainfall deficits
  from **OND 2015**, worst **Jan–Apr 2016**, recovery Apr–Aug 2016 (Palau's
  accumulated deficit bottomed −85.73 in in Aug 2016).
- **Rainfall (% normal):** Q4 2015 — Majuro 39%, Yap 40%, Palau 46%, Kosrae 52%;
  Q1 2016 — Kwajalein 18%, Majuro 27%, Yap 35%, Palau 55%. Koror got ~1/3 of normal
  from 1 Mar 2016 and its lowest 4-month total since 1951.
- **Gradient:** west + north hardest (Palau, Yap, northern RMI atolls); Chuuk less
  affected; Pohnpei/Kosrae had big relative shortfalls from a very wet baseline;
  American Samoa was anomalously *wet* (127–137%).
- **Crisis:** FSM emergency 18 Feb 2016; RMI emergency 3 Feb → disaster 8/10 Mar
  (extended twice); **Obama FEMA disaster declaration for RMI 27 Apr 2016** (only
  FAS country to request international aid); Palau EO-389 21/22 Mar 2016 — Ngerimel
  Dam dried, Koror/Airai on 3 hr/day rationing; ~21,000 affected in RMI, ~100,000
  in FSM; RMI deployed 55 RO units / 33 communities.

### 2023–24 (strong, but weaker than the two above)
- **Timing:** declared Jul 2023, peak Dec 2023–Jan 2024 (ONI ~+2.0 °C), neutral by
  mid-2024.
- **Impact:** **FSM national emergency 11 Mar 2024** (~22,700 people, ~20% of
  population; Yap/Ulithi reached **D4 exceptional drought**; Yap's Mar–Apr rain was
  2nd-driest in 73 years). **RMI** severe drought ~5,200 people (Wotje D4) but **no
  national disaster declaration**. **Palau mostly spared** (Stage 1 / abnormal
  dryness only).
- **Comparison:** FSM/SPREP: impacts "unlikely to match" 1997–98 or 2015–16.

## Which areas are most affected, and why

| Tier | Areas | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Most vulnerable** | Northern RMI atolls (Wotje, Utrik, Ailuk, Mejit, Likiep, Maloelap, Wotho, Ujae…); FSM outer atolls (Yap's Ulithi/Woleai/Fais; Chuuk's Mortlocks; Pohnpei's southern atolls); Palau's Kayangel/Sonsorol/Hatohobei | No surface water; thin lens; rain catchment only. RMI: 55% of islands have lens <2.5 m; islands <300 m wide fully depleted in 1998 drought (Barkey & Bailey 2017). |
| **Driest high island** | Yap Proper (~122 in/yr; streams dry ~3 mo/yr) vs Pohnpei (~190 in/yr) | Lower baseline rainfall; intermittent streams. |
| **More resilient** | Pohnpei, Kosrae, Chuuk Lagoon high islands, Palau's Babeldaob | Rainfall + perennial streams/rivers (Babeldaob has Palau's only perennial rivers + Lake Ngardok). |
| **Surprising failure point** | Palau's Koror (limestone, dam-dependent) | Ngerimel Dam dried up in 2016 despite national streams. |

## Population exposure (approximate, flagged as such)

- RMI ~55–58,000 total; Majuro ~20,000+; 2013 northern-atoll drought ~6,400 people
  / 15 atolls; 2024 ~5,200 (12.3%) severe + ~14,600–15,000 (~35%) drought+inundation.
- FSM ~102,000–113,815; 2024 drought >21,000 urgent need; Yap State ~6,800–10,000
  (outer islands ~2,737).
- Palau ~18,000; five outlying states ~1,350.
