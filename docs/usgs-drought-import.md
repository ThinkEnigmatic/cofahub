# USGS western-Pacific drought dataset — import & method verification

Retrieved the authoritative USGS data release (public domain) through a real
Chrome session (Cloudflare-gated on ScienceBase; headless browsers are blocked),
and used it to verify the app's drought classification *exactly*.

## Dataset

- **Title:** Drought Monitoring and 7-Day Precipitation Forecasting for western
  Pacific nations from 2000–2026 (child of "Hydro-climatic datasets…", Senay,
  Kagone, Dwomoh & Andersen 2026).
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5066/P1RXUMV3 (CC0-1.0 public domain).
- **ScienceBase item:** `69f8f726b66b014d9c576c9c`.

## Verified drought-classification method (from `3_IMERG_Monthly_Drought_Monitor.R`)

Four indices, all computed against the **2000–2024 baseline**:

1. **1-/3-/6-Month Rainfall Index** — quintile class of the monthly precipitation
   and its right-aligned rolling 3-/6-month sums, each vs the same-calendar-month
   distribution: `<20th→Drier, <40th→Dry, <60th→Typical, <80th→Wet, ≥80th→Wetter`.
2. **PEAC 4/8-inch Index** — absolute traffic light on the latest month:
   `<101.6 mm→Red (drought concern), 101.6–203.2 mm→Yellow (adequate),
   >203.2 mm→Dark Green (sufficient)`.

The app's `drought/percentiles.quintile_index` and `drought/percentiles.peac_index`
reproduce these thresholds exactly; `drought/monitor.py` computes all four indices.

## Notes / differences

- The USGS pipeline runs on **IMERG Early Run**; the app computes the same
  indicators from **ERA5/CHIRPS** (more reliable over ocean-adjacent atolls).
- The USGS quintile baseline is **2000–2024**; the app defaults to **1991–2020**
  (both ~25-year climatologies — noted in the UI).
- The raw monthly CSV is generated by the R pipeline from IMERG (not shipped as a
  standalone file); the app reproduces it live rather than importing a snapshot.
